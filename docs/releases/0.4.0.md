### Play and presentation

- Frames are cheaper to draw, pixel for pixel the same: the software GPU renders gameplay about a third faster, and a second pass takes a further 27–34% of the CPU off it. Levels load in about half the CPU time.
- A disc that will not open says why and what to do: a missing track `.bin` is named, and a folder, an archive, the PC game or another PlayStation game each get their own message. A `.cue` dropped on the program is taken as the disc, and a bad command-line option or value is named in one line instead of misread.
- In a split screen the pause menu belongs to whoever paused, every missing controller is asked for in turn, each pane's death roll runs at the console's rate, and player one's controller style survives a turn on the keyboard.
- Fading items and bodies are see-through ghosts, as on the console, rather than dimmed solids. Shards of a broken pane or crate wear its surface and tumble as they fly, and a teleporter fades the gun to black and back over the console's four frames.

### Gameplay

- Shots go where you are looking when you look up or down. Since 0.3.0 the shots had the console's pitch and the picture the opposite one, so looking up at the ceiling sent every bolt into the floor. The view, the gun and the controls now share the console's sign; the stick styles' default inversion follows the console's too.
- Zone gates behave as the console's. A creature left alive in another zone comes back freshly spawned from the console's compact record, with its health rounded and its hunt forgotten. A body comes back lying on its death clip's last frame, and gibs and dropped items stay behind.
- Every shot leaves from the console's muzzle point to the unit, checked against the disc's own code for every weapon on both discs. Bodies block and stop shots from the console's own actor list, last frame's in slot order, and a bullet that stops in a body always draws blood.
- The hand grenade follows Grenade3's think: a fuse that runs out throws the grenade rather than bursting it in the hand, a grenade primed in a dying hand vanishes with it, and the throw's rumble and sound come in the console's order. A grenade or BFG ball thrown into a wall starts on the thrower's side of it.
- BOSS2's rotating panels turn a quarter turn per pulse, JAIL2's flickering light is lit only in the zone that has it, lifts sweep only the frame they can reach you, and teleporter arrivals keep your momentum and heading where the console does.
- Saves taken during a teleporter fade restore the player's light. Saves from 0.3.0 still load.

### Tools and validation

- New reference generators run the disc's own code for the muzzle, the railgun's ring and the actor list on both discs; the port's tests match every case. `raster_fastpath` checks the new raster loops against the general ones.
- A whole-port sanitizer sweep (MSVC AddressSanitizer, GCC and Clang UBSan/ASan, Clang MemorySanitizer) over the suite and the headless disc sweep on both discs is clean; its three real findings are fixed.
- The changes in this release were checked against the unit tests, the disc-backed client suites on both discs, and the Windows (MSVC) and Linux (GCC and Clang) builds. The release archives are built and tested on Windows x64, Linux x64 and macOS Apple silicon, with SHA-256 checksums.

### Known limitations

The 1.0 boundary in `docs/RELEASING.md` was signed off on 6 November: measurements that need retail hardware, such as GPU timing, final audio levels, movie A/V phase and physical controllers, qualify the port after 1.0 and have not been made. Deliberate differences from the console are listed in the player guide.
